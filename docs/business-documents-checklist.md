# Documents & Compliance Checklist for a New Food Business Owner (India)

> Research reference for a possible Praang feature — not legal advice, and not yet built into the app. See the caveats section at the bottom before turning any of this into an in-app template library.

## The weak point in this plan, stated up front

Legal document templates are jurisdiction- and time-sensitive, and several of the items below (POSH policy, appointment letters, franchise agreements) carry real liability if the template is wrong, outdated, or missing a state-specific clause. Handing a restaurant owner a generic "appointment letter" or "franchise agreement" template inside software, with no lawyer review and no update mechanism when labour codes or state rules change, is a way to give someone false confidence right before they get into legal trouble. If this becomes a real Praang feature, it needs either a lawyer-reviewed template set with a visible "last reviewed" date and a disclaimer, or a partnership with an actual legal service — not just text generated once and left static in the app. Flagging this now because it's cheap to design around and expensive to fix after an owner has already relied on a bad template.

With that said, here's the full list.

---

## 1. Business Registration & Structure

- **Business structure decision**: sole proprietorship, partnership, LLP, or private limited company — this choice determines which of the documents below even apply.
- **PAN (business)** — required for any structure beyond a sole proprietorship using the owner's personal PAN.
- **Partnership Deed** (if partnership) or **LLP Agreement** (if LLP) or **MOA/AOA + Certificate of Incorporation** (if Pvt Ltd).
- **Shareholders' Agreement** (if Pvt Ltd with more than one investor).
- **Udyam/MSME Registration** — optional but unlocks collateral-free loans, subsidy schemes, and delayed-payment protection from larger buyers.
- **Current bank account** opening documents (KYC, board resolution if a company).

## 2. Food-Specific Licenses & Regulatory Registrations

- **FSSAI Registration or License** — mandatory for every food business operator in India, tiered by turnover:
  - Basic Registration: turnover under ₹12 lakh/year
  - State License: turnover ₹12 lakh – ₹20 crore/year
  - Central License: turnover above ₹20 crore/year, or any business with import/export
- **Health/Trade License** from the local municipal body — still required in many cities, though some (e.g. Delhi in 2025) have folded this into the FSSAI license. Verify locally; don't assume it's gone everywhere.
- **Eating House License** — required specifically for restaurants/eateries in most states, issued by local police/municipal authority, separate from FSSAI.
- **Fire Safety NOC** — mandatory for any commercial kitchen; renewed periodically.
- **Liquor License** (if serving alcohol) — state excise department, separate application and much longer process.
- **Music License** (PPL/IPRS/Novex) — legally required if playing recorded music in the outlet, frequently ignored, actually enforced via infringement notices.
- **Pollution Control / Environmental Clearance** — depends on kitchen scale and local rules, more relevant to larger commissary/cloud-kitchen operations.
- **Signage/Hoarding License** — municipal permission for exterior signage.
- **Weights & Measures (Legal Metrology) registration** — if selling packaged items by weight/volume.

## 3. Premises & Local Municipal

- **Shops and Establishments Act Registration** — with the local labour department; baseline requirement for any commercial premises with employees.
- **Rent Agreement / Lease Deed** — for the outlet premises, should include a clause on FSSAI/license transferability if the business is sold.
- **Building/Occupancy Certificate** (for owned premises) or landlord's NOC for commercial use (for rented).
- **Trade License** from the municipal corporation.

## 4. Tax & Financial Compliance

- **GST Registration** — mandatory above ₹20 lakh annual turnover (₹10 lakh in some special-category states), and mandatory from day one for e-commerce/delivery-platform listing regardless of turnover.
- **GST return filing schedule** (monthly/quarterly depending on scheme — composition vs regular).
- **TDS registration (TAN)** — once the business starts paying salaries or vendor invoices above threshold.
- **Professional Tax registration** (state-specific, applies in Maharashtra, Karnataka, MP among others).
- **Books of accounts / bookkeeping system** — statutory requirement, not just good practice.
- **Import Export Code (IEC)** — only if importing specialty ingredients/equipment.

## 5. HR & Employment Documents

Under the new Labour Codes, a written appointment letter is now mandatory for every employee regardless of business size — this used to be a good practice, it's now a compliance requirement.

- **Offer Letter**
- **Appointment Letter** — must cover role, wages, hours, leave, and reference the POSH policy
- **Employment Contract / Terms of Employment**
- **Confidentiality / NDA Agreement** — for any staff with access to recipes, costing, or vendor pricing (directly relevant given Praang's own recipe-costing data)
- **Non-Compete / Non-Solicitation clause** — limited enforceability in India but still commonly included
- **Joining Letter / Confirmation Letter** (post-probation)
- **Relieving Letter** and **Experience Letter** (on exit)
- **POSH Policy** — legally mandatory once the establishment has 10 or more employees (the Act draws no line by industry or revenue past that threshold); must name Internal Committee members, define the complaint process, and be acknowledged in writing by every employee including contract/part-time staff
- **Employee Handbook / Code of Conduct**
- **Leave Policy**, **Attendance Policy**, **Wages & Overtime Policy**, **Grievance Redressal Policy** — effectively mandatory in writing under the Shops & Establishments Act and Labour Codes
- **PF Registration** — mandatory once headcount reaches 20 employees (register within 30 days of crossing the threshold)
- **ESI Registration** — mandatory once headcount reaches 10 employees in most states, for employees earning up to ₹21,000/month
- **Salary Slip template**, **Form 16 / Form 12BA** at year-end

## 6. Vendor, Partner & Platform Documents

- **Vendor/Supplier Agreement** — pricing, delivery terms, quality standards, payment cycle
- **NDA with vendors** — where recipe/costing data is shared for procurement
- **Purchase Order template**
- **Delivery Platform Agreement** (Swiggy/Zomato/etc.) — read the commission and delisting clauses closely before signing
- **Insurance**: fire, public liability (customer injury on premises), and increasingly, cyber/data insurance if handling customer payment data

## 7. Franchise & Multi-Outlet Expansion (once the business is ready to scale)

- **Trademark Registration** — do this *before* franchising. FSSAI registration does not give you brand ownership; without a registered trademark, you can't legally stop a franchisee (or a copycat) from using your name after a falling-out.
- **Franchise Disclosure Document (FDD)** — not legally mandated in India the way it is in the US, but any credible franchisor provides one: financials, litigation history, obligations, territory rights.
- **Franchise Agreement** — term, fees, royalty %, territory exclusivity, quality control rights, termination conditions.
- **Trademark License / Registered User Agreement** — recorded with the Trademark Registry under Section 49 of the Trade Marks Act so the franchisee has legal standing to use the mark.
- **Area Development Agreement** — if one franchisee is being given rights to open multiple outlets in a territory.
- **Master Franchise Agreement** — if appointing a regional master franchisee rather than dealing with each outlet directly.
- **Operations Manual** — this is where Praang's own SOP fields (recipes, cut styles, heat, timing, do's/don'ts) directly feed into a franchise pack; a franchisor without a documented ops manual has a much weaker legal and practical position when enforcing quality standards.

Important flag: franchising across Indian states is not "sign once, done everywhere" — Shops & Establishments rules, food licensing, and stamp duty all vary by state, so a single template stamped correctly in Madhya Pradesh may be under-stamped or non-compliant in another state. Multi-state expansion needs state-specific legal review, not a copy-paste of the same agreement.

## 8. Operational SOPs & Training Content

This is the category Praang is already building toward (recipe SOPs — cut style, heat, timing, do's/don'ts, cooking technique):

- Recipe SOPs (per dish) — already in progress
- Kitchen hygiene & food safety checklist (ties to FSSAI compliance, not just good practice — inspectors check for this)
- Opening/closing checklists per outlet
- Staff onboarding & training checklist
- Customer complaint handling SOP
- Waste management & disposal log
- Equipment maintenance schedule
- Fire safety drill record

## 9. Business Growth / Education Content (not documents, but learning material)

Things a first-time owner typically doesn't know to look for, that could be short guides rather than legal templates:

- Unit economics: how to actually calculate whether an outlet is profitable (food cost %, labour cost %, rent as % of revenue — benchmarks vary by format)
- Menu engineering: which dishes to promote based on margin vs popularity (Praang's recipe costing data feeds this directly)
- When to franchise vs open a second company-owned outlet
- Cash flow management for a seasonal/high-perishable-inventory business
- Common first-outlet mistakes (over-hiring before revenue proves out, under-pricing menu items, ignoring FSSAI renewal dates)

---

## If this becomes an actual Praang feature

Recommend scoping it as three separate, honestly-labeled tiers rather than one "legal documents" bucket:

1. **Checklists** (like this one) — safe to ship as-is, no liability, just informational.
2. **Fillable templates** (appointment letter, NDA, vendor agreement) — needs a lawyer to draft/review at least once, a visible "last reviewed" date, and a clear disclaimer that it's a starting point, not a substitute for legal counsel.
3. **Franchise/expansion documents** — probably shouldn't be a static in-app template at all; too bespoke and too high-stakes. Better as a "talk to a lawyer, here's what to bring" checklist than a fill-in-the-blanks form.
